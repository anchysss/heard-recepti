HEARD project

Life-saving help, hidden inside an ordinary recipe app.

Recipes looks like a calm cooking app. Underneath is a quiet safety layer: voice SOS, trusted contacts, encrypted recordings, location, AI support and community.

Download demo See features
Why it exists

Giving the silenced a way to be heard before help is too late.

HEARD is designed for moments when a person cannot explain, type, call, or ask for help in front of an abuser. The app keeps the appearance of everyday life while quietly activating a prepared safety flow.

App capabilities

What Recipes can do

01

Voice SOS

A wake word can start the SOS flow even when the app is not open, using offline Vosk recognition.

02

Direct call attempt

The app tries the configured emergency number if Android allows it, then falls back to a trusted contact or dialer.

03

Trusted contacts

Multiple trusted people can receive location SMS and serve as a no-tap call fallback.

04

Encrypted evidence

Audio/video recording can start from SOS or manually, then stores encrypted files with timestamps and SHA-256 integrity metadata.

05

Disguised entry

The outside is a recipe list. The hidden area opens with a secret phrase, PIN, or voice code.

06

AI support

A trauma-informed chat demonstrates how calm, concrete support could work during crisis.

07

Community

The demo includes group rooms and one-to-one chat as a base for a support network.

08

EN and SR modes

English is the default. US/SR modes change wake words, emergency numbers, resources and AI guidance.

Current demo

The demo proves the flow without pretending to be production-ready.

The Android demo shows the core idea: disguise, wake word, trusted contacts, encrypted recordings, SOS SMS, AI conversation, community and US/SR help resources.

Intentionally limited before deployment

  • Emergency auto-calling still depends on Android, carrier and regional restrictions.
  • The AI chat uses an available free/demo configuration; production should use a stronger paid model such as Anthropic.
  • Peer chat still needs production verification, moderation and end-to-end encryption.
  • A real iPhone/TestFlight build requires a native iOS app and Apple signing; it is not generated from the Android APK.
  • Clinical, legal and trauma-informed validation must happen before public use.

Production upgrades

The path to real no-tap help

Backend alert + response calls

The phone sends a silent SOS signal to a backend, and the backend calls trusted contacts or a 24/7 response line.

Regulated emergency dispatch

For the US: explore Twilio E911 where supported, RapidSOS/Noonlight, or similar dispatch partners.

Premium AI support

Production should use a stronger paid model such as Anthropic, reviewed by trauma specialists.

Verified support network

The deployment plan is for NGOs, counselors and safe centers to become verified support nodes.

Legal-grade evidence export

Recording, encryption, timestamps and hashes work in the demo; legal-grade export needs expert forensic/legal review.

iOS and wearable signal

A separate iOS strategy, Apple Watch panic gesture and check-in flows that respect Apple restrictions.
