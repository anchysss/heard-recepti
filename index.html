HEARD

A quiet way to ask for help when speaking is unsafe.

Recipes looks like a calm cooking app. Underneath, it can help someone alert trusted people, record what is happening, find support and keep the real purpose hidden.

Download demo See features
Why it exists

For people who cannot safely say what is happening.

HEARD is built for moments when asking for help out loud could make things worse. The app keeps the appearance of everyday life while quietly starting a prepared safety plan.

What it helps with

How Recipes can protect someone

01

SOS by voice

A chosen phrase can start the help plan when opening the hidden screen would be dangerous.

02

Calls for help

The app can try the emergency number first, then a trusted person if the phone blocks that call.

03

Trusted contacts

Add more than one person who can receive an alert and check on the survivor quickly.

04

Private evidence

Recording can start during SOS or manually. Files are protected and marked with time and integrity checks.

05

Hidden as recipes

The outside looks ordinary. The safety area opens only with a secret phrase, PIN or voice code.

06

Calm AI chat

The demo shows how a helper could offer clear, steady next steps when someone feels overwhelmed.

07

Support community

Group rooms and one-to-one chat show how a safer support network could grow around the app.

08

English and Serbian

English is the default. Language mode changes help numbers, links, phrases and guidance.

Current demo

The demo shows the life-saving idea clearly, without pretending it is finished.

It shows the main experience: a normal-looking recipe app, a hidden safety area, trusted people, SOS alerts, protected recordings, support chat and help resources.

What is intentionally limited for now

  • Emergency calling depends on phone rules, the carrier and the country. The app cannot override those rules.
  • The AI chat uses a lower-cost demo setup. A real service should use a stronger paid model reviewed by specialists.
  • Community chat needs moderation, safety rules and stronger protection before real users rely on it.
  • The iPhone file is a demo artifact. Real iPhone installation needs Apple signing or TestFlight.
  • Legal, clinical and survivor-safety review must happen before public use.

Next steps

What is needed before real-world use

A response line that can call back

When SOS starts, a trusted service could call the person, alert contacts and keep the case moving.

Emergency partners

For the US, the project should work with approved emergency and safety partners before promising direct police dispatch.

Stronger crisis support

A real service should use a stronger paid AI model and have its responses reviewed by trauma specialists.

Verified support network

NGOs, counselors and safe centers can become trusted support points with clear roles and accountability.

Evidence package for professionals

The demo already protects recordings and marks when they were made. Legal use needs an expert-reviewed export process.

iPhone and watch safety signals

An iPhone path should include Apple Watch signals, check-ins and Apple-approved safety flows.
