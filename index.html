HEARD

A voice when violence tries to silence you.

HEARD hides inside an ordinary recipe app, but it is built for the moment when a victim cannot safely touch the phone. Her own secret words can start SOS from the background, alert the trusted people she chose, and send where she is.

Download demo See how help starts
Why it exists

Because visible help can make danger worse.

In an attack, the most dangerous seconds can be the seconds spent reaching for a phone. If the attacker sees a call screen, a police number, or a panic button, violence can escalate before help arrives. HEARD gives the victim another route: a voice route, hidden behind recipes, that can call for help without making the danger visible.

How it helps

HEARD is not one button. It is a quiet chain of help built around the victim.

Each victim defines her own safety words and her own trusted people in advance. When danger starts, she does not need to search, unlock, tap through screens, or explain herself. The words become the signal.

01

Immediate danger

When she is being attacked and cannot reach the phone

The phone can be across the room, on the counter, in a bag, or in the attacker’s hand. If it can hear her safety phrase, HEARD can start SOS without her touching the screen.

  • No searching for contacts during panic.
  • No visible emergency button that can provoke the attacker.
  • The phrase is personal, chosen by the victim, not a universal command everyone knows.
02

Silent SOS

When the attacker must not know help has been called

From the outside, the app is still Recipes. Behind that disguise, SOS can notify the trusted people she selected and send them her GPS location, so help starts moving while the attacker sees nothing unusual.

  • Trusted contacts are chosen by the victim herself.
  • SOS can send SMS alerts with location instead of waiting for a typed message.
  • Future emergency integrations can add direct police pathways where the law and phone systems allow it.
03

Background listening

When the app is closed, hidden, or not on screen

HEARD is designed to keep listening in the background after setup. The victim should not have to open the app, unlock the hidden area, or tap a crisis button while someone is hurting her.

  • The recipe screen protects the app’s real purpose.
  • The safety phrase can work while the phone is not in her hand.
  • The goal is help without extra steps in the moment of danger.
04

If the attacker appears

When the real screen must disappear immediately

If the victim is inside the hidden safety area and the attacker comes back, shaking the phone can instantly leave the real app and return to ordinary recipes. The phone stops looking like evidence, support, or an SOS tool.

  • No need to find a close button or calmly navigate back.
  • The disguise is restored with a quick physical movement.
  • It protects the victim in the moment when being discovered could trigger more violence.
05

When the phone is not reachable

When the phone is far away, taken, or unsafe to touch

Abuse often includes control of the phone. HEARD treats voice as the shortcut: if the device is nearby and listening, the victim can ask for help without having the phone in her hands.

  • Useful when the phone is on a table, under furniture, charging, or taken away.
  • Built for moments when moving toward the phone would increase danger.
  • Designed so the signal starts from words, not from touch.
06

Long-term abuse

When she is not ready to report, leave, or tell anyone yet

Many victims survive for months or years before they can act. HEARD can give quiet support, safety information, AI-guided conversation in the demo, and survivor-to-survivor connection so she hears: this is real, and you are not alone.

  • A private place to name what is happening.
  • Resources and support without making the phone look like a crisis app.
  • A path toward help before the victim is ready for a formal report.
07

Evidence without exposure

When she needs proof, but the abuser must not know

For the person thinking: “My ribs are bruised, not broken, so I do not have enough proof to report it, even though it hurts as if they were broken.” HEARD can start protected recording and preserve what happened quietly.

  • Encrypted recordings keep sensitive material protected.
  • Timestamps and chain-of-custody records help show when evidence was created.
  • The goal is evidence a lawyer, advocate, or investigator can review without forcing the victim to expose herself during the attack.

Current demo

The demo shows the core promise: a victim’s words can become action.

Today it demonstrates the main safety paths: personal secret words, background listening, SOS to trusted contacts, SMS with GPS location, shake-to-hide return to recipes, hidden recording, support conversation, community and help resources. It is a demo, not a public emergency service yet.

What is intentionally limited until real deployment

  • The demo is for review and testing, not a replacement for emergency services today.
  • Direct emergency calls depend on phone rules, carriers and country laws; production needs approved partners.
  • AI support is only a demo level helper for now; a real service needs stronger models and expert review.
  • Community spaces must have trained moderation and strict safety rules before real victims use them.
  • Evidence export must be reviewed by legal and forensic professionals before it is used in real cases.

Next steps

What turns the demo into real protection

A real response network

When SOS starts, trained responders can alert trusted people, call back safely and keep the situation moving toward help.

Police and emergency pathways

Direct police help must be built with approved emergency partners, so the promise is real and lawful.

Human-centered crisis support

The AI helper should be stronger, safer and reviewed by trauma specialists, with human escalation when needed.

Verified support network

NGOs, counselors and safe centers can become trusted support points with clear roles and accountability.

Evidence that professionals can trust

The app already points toward protected recordings; the next step is a professional evidence package that courts and advocates can review.

iPhone and wearable signals

A real iPhone path should include watch signals, safety check-ins and Apple-approved ways to ask for help.
