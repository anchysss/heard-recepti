HEARD

Help can start before the attacker knows.

Recipes looks like an ordinary cooking app. Underneath, HEARD is built for the moment when someone is being attacked, beaten or threatened and cannot safely pick up the phone to call for help.

Download demo See what it solves
Why it exists

When a visible call for help could make the violence worse.

Many victims do not have the luxury of unlocking a phone, searching for police and tapping call. If the attacker sees the call, the danger can escalate in seconds. HEARD is designed to send the signal quietly, so help can begin while the phone still looks harmless.

What it solves in real life

HEARD is built around the moments victims actually face

01

When the attack is happening now

If the victim cannot tap, type or visibly call, a spoken phrase can start the help flow.

02

The attacker does not see the alarm

The app is disguised as recipes, because the safest call for help is often the one the attacker never notices.

03

Trusted people are alerted

More than one trusted person can be prepared to receive an alert and check where the victim is.

04

Evidence is collected quietly

Recording can start without the abuser knowing, so the victim is not left with only fear and memory.

05

For victims not ready to leave yet

Support, planning and conversation can exist quietly until the person is ready for the next step.

06

“I do not have enough proof”

For the person thinking: “My ribs are bruised, not broken, so I do not have enough proof to report it, even though it hurts as if they were broken.”

07

You are not alone

A victim can speak with support and, later, with other victims who understand the fear, shame and hesitation.

08

English and Serbian modes point people toward the right numbers, links and local support path.

Current demo

This demo is about one urgent question: can help start without making the victim more visible?

The current version demonstrates the core life-saving path: disguise, silent SOS intent, trusted people, protected recordings, support conversation, community and practical help resources.

What is intentionally limited until real deployment

  • The demo is for review and testing, not a replacement for emergency services today.
  • Direct emergency calls depend on phone rules, carriers and country laws; production needs approved partners.
  • AI support is only a demo level helper for now; a real service needs stronger models and expert review.
  • Community spaces must have trained moderation and strict safety rules before real victims use them.
  • Evidence export must be reviewed by legal and forensic professionals before it is used in real cases.

Next steps

What turns the demo into real protection

A real response network

When SOS starts, trained responders can alert trusted people, call back safely and keep the situation moving toward help.

Police and emergency pathways

Direct police help must be built with approved emergency partners, so the promise is real and lawful.

Human-centered crisis support

The AI helper should be stronger, safer and reviewed by trauma specialists, with human escalation when needed.

Verified support network

NGOs, counselors and safe centers can become trusted support points with clear roles and accountability.

Evidence that professionals can trust

The app already points toward protected recordings; the next step is a professional evidence package that courts and advocates can review.

iPhone and wearable signals

A real iPhone path should include watch signals, safety check-ins and Apple-approved ways to ask for help.
